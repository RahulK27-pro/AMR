# Architectural Analysis: AMR Mapping & Exploration

Based on a detailed codebase review by my expert subagents, here is a comprehensive breakdown of your robotic system's mapping and navigation architecture, including an explanation of the RViz visualizations and the root cause of the robot freezing.

## 1. How the Map is Built (SLAM Toolbox)
The foundation of the exploration phase is the `slam_toolbox` node (running in online, asynchronous mapping mode).

* **Map Generation**: SLAM Toolbox listens to the LiDAR (`/scan`) and the fused odometry (`odom -> base_link`). It aligns incoming laser scans against previous scans using a pose graph optimization algorithm (`CeresSolver`). 
* **Dynamic Sizing**: It periodically publishes the live map to the `/map` topic as a 2D grid. The map automatically expands its boundaries as the robot discovers new areas.
* **How it is Stored**: Once you are satisfied with the map, your `save_map.sh` script triggers the `map_saver_cli` node. This node listens to the `/map` topic one last time and writes it to disk as two files: a `.pgm` image (pixel grid) and a `.yaml` file (metadata regarding resolution and origin).

## 2. How Motion Planning and Exploration Works
Autonomous exploration is a coordinated effort between **`explore_lite`** and **Nav2**.

* **Finding Frontiers (`explore_lite`)**: This node analyzes the global costmap and looks for "frontiers"—the exact boundary line between known free space and unknown space. It scores frontiers based on their distance and the size of the unknown region behind them, and sends a navigation goal to the highest-scoring frontier.
* **Global Planner (`NavfnPlanner`)**: When it receives the goal, the global planner calculates the shortest path from the robot to the frontier. Crucially, it is configured with `allow_unknown: true`, which allows it to plot paths directly into unmapped territory.
* **Local Planner (DWB or MPPI)**: The local planner's job is to follow the global path while avoiding dynamic obstacles. It rapidly simulates dozens (or thousands, for MPPI) of short trajectories into the immediate future. It scores each trajectory using "critics" (e.g., how close it stays to the global path, how well it avoids obstacles) and executes the best one by sending velocity commands to `/cmd_vel`.

## 3. What You Are Seeing in RViz (The Colors)
RViz visualizes the raw data streams passing through ROS 2. Here is what you are looking at in your image:

* **Gray / White / Black Canvas**: This is the raw SLAM map.
  * **White**: Known, safe free space.
  * **Black Pixels**: Lethal obstacles (walls).
  * **Gray**: Unexplored, unknown space.
* **Green Spheres and Purple/Blue Squares**: These are markers generated by `explore_lite`. The squares highlight the actual frontier boundary cells, and the large green spheres represent the specific target points `explore_lite` wants the robot to drive to.
* **Cyan / Red / Dark Blue Halos**: This is the **Costmap Inflation Layer**. Nav2 creates a "buffer zone" around obstacles to ensure the robot doesn't scrape the walls. The colors represent a gradient of danger: red is lethal (collision), cyan is highly dangerous, and dark blue is less optimal but traversable. 
* **Orange/Yellow Line**: This is the **Global Path** generated by `NavfnPlanner`, showing the intended route to the frontier.
* **Blue Line (coming from robot)**: If visible, this is the **Local Trajectory** generated by the local planner, predicting exactly where the robot will be in the next 1-2 seconds.

## 4. Why the Robot is Getting Stuck (The Conflict)
You noticed the robot successfully receives an orange path, turns toward it, but then freezes without moving forward. This is caused by a mathematical conflict between the robot's physical size, `explore_lite`, and the local planner:

> [!WARNING]
> **The Root Cause:** `explore_lite` is commanding the robot to drive through gaps that are too small, and the local planner is freezing out of self-preservation.

1. **The Impossible Goal**: The robot has a physical diameter of roughly **48cm**. However, `explore_lite` is configured with `min_frontier_size: 0.25` (25cm). This means `explore_lite` frequently targets narrow gaps or tight corners that the robot physically cannot fit through.
2. **The Blind Global Path**: Because `NavfnPlanner` is allowed to plan through unknown space, it blindly draws the orange line straight through these narrow gaps, completely unaware that the walls extend into the unknown.
3. **Local Planner Paralysis**: As the robot turns to follow the path, its LiDAR spots the tight walls. The local costmap instantly inflates those walls. The local planner evaluates the orange path and realizes that moving forward will cause a collision with the inflation layer. Because its "critics" heavily penalize deviating from the orange path, it cannot find an alternative route. **Instead of crashing, it commands zero forward velocity.**
4. **The Timeout**: The robot spins in place, trapped between a global planner telling it to go forward and a local planner telling it to stop. It waits there until `explore_lite` hits its 30-second `progress_timeout`, gives up on that frontier, and tries another one.

### The Solution (Already Implemented)
To fix this, we previously swapped the local planner from the rigid **DWB** to the highly advanced **MPPI**. MPPI evaluates 2,000 trajectories at once and is much better at smoothly deviating from impossible paths or quickly realizing a path is blocked, preventing the robot from freezing in place indefinitely. We also fixed the global costmap layer to ensure `explore_lite` accurately assesses the frontiers.
